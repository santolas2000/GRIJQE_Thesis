%\subsection{Calculating projections of $X^{\alpha \beta}$}

The newly-computed components of the $X^{\alpha \beta}$ tensor are now used to compute the projections needed to calculate $\hat{q}$ and $\diff E / \diff x$ (Eq. (\ref{eq:collision_from_tensors})) -- $v^\alpha v^\beta X^{\alpha \beta}$ and $\delta^{\alpha \beta} X^{\alpha \beta}$. The projections are calculated excluding any terms proportional to $\delta^{ij}\varepsilon^{mn}$ and $\varepsilon^{ij}\delta^{mn}$, as they become $0$ when contracted with the general correlator expression (Eqs. (\ref{eq:correlator_contractions_null1}) and (\ref{eq:correlator_contractions_null2})). The $v^\alpha v^\beta X^{\alpha \beta}$ projection, for example, can be simplified into a surprisingly compact form, considering the amount of terms summed:

\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
v^\alpha v^\beta X^{\alpha \beta} = \frac{g^4}{2N_c} & \int_0^t \diff t' \int \frac{\diff^2 \mathbf{k_\perp}}{(2\pi)^2}
\int \frac{\diff^2 \mathbf{l}_\perp}{(2\pi)^2}
\int \diff^2 \mathbf{u}_\perp
 \int \diff^2 \mathbf{w}_\perp  
\\
& \left[ (v^1)^2 J_0 (k \tau) J_0(l\tau') \delta^{ij} \delta^{mn} - \left(\frac{\mathbf{v}_\perp \times \mathbf{k}_\perp}{k}\frac{\mathbf{v}_\perp \times \mathbf{l}_\perp}{l}\right) J_1 (k \tau) J_1(l\tau') \varepsilon^{ij} \varepsilon^{mn} \right]
\\
& e^{i \mathbf{k}_\perp \cdot (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{u})_\perp}
e^{i \mathbf{l}_\perp \cdot (\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{w})_\perp}
f^{bca} f^{dea}
 \big< \alpha^i_{1b} (\mathbf{u}_\perp) \alpha^m_{1d} (\mathbf{w}_\perp) \big>
 \big< \alpha^j_{2c} (\mathbf{u}_\perp) \alpha^n_{2e} (\mathbf{w}_\perp) \big>
\end{split} 
\end{equation}
the $( \delta^{\alpha \beta} - v^\alpha v^\beta / v^2)X^{\alpha \beta}$ projection has a similar structure, differing by the terms in square brackets, which nonetheless also contain Bessel functions of degrees $0$ and $1$ with arguments $k\tau$ and $l\tau'$. 

These projections must now be simplified analytically to the greatest extent possible. In Appendix \ref{ap:Projections}, a procedure which allows reducing the projections to triple integrals, which then may be numerically computed, is written out in detail; the integral representations of the Bessel functions, the orthogonality of the Bessel functions, and the general form for the gluon field correlator in Eq. (\ref{eq:general_correlator}) are used.


\section{Expressions for $\hat{q}$ and $\diff E / \diff x$}


Combining the results (Eqs. (\ref{eq:simplified_unintegrated_projections})) with the expressions for $\hat{q}$ and $\diff E / \diff x$ of Eqs. (\ref{eq:collision_from_tensors}),

\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
\begin{split}
	\hat{q} & = \frac{2}{v} \left(\delta^{\alpha \beta} - \frac{v^\alpha v^\beta}{v^2} \right) X^{\alpha\beta} \\
	& = \frac{g^4}{2}  ({N_c}^2 - 1) \int_0^t \diff t' \int_0^{2\pi}  \frac{\diff \phi}{2\pi}  \int_0^{2\pi} \frac{\diff \varphi}{2\pi} \\
& \qquad \frac{1}{v}\Bigg[ \frac{1+v^2}{v^2} G_1 (r) G_2 (r)  \Big( (v^1)^2 \cos(\phi-\varphi) + {v_\perp}^2 (\cos \phi \cos \varphi+1) \Big) \\
& \qquad \quad - \frac{1-v^2}{v^2} h_1(r) h_2(r) \Big( (v^1)^2 \cos (\phi-\varphi) + {v_\perp}^2 (\cos \phi \cos \varphi -1) \Big) \\
& \qquad \quad - 2 v_\perp h_1(r) h_2(r) (\cos \phi + \cos \varphi ) \Bigg]
\end{split} \label{eq:final_full_qhat} \\
\begin{split}
	\frac{\diff E}{\diff x} &= - \frac{v}{T}\frac{v^\alpha v^\beta}{v^2} X^{\alpha \beta} \\
	& = -\frac{1}{vT} \frac{g^4}{4} (N_c-1)^2  \int_0^t \diff t' \int_0^{2\pi} \frac{\diff \phi}{2\pi} \int_0^{2\pi} \frac{\diff \varphi}{2\pi}
  \Bigg[ (v^1)^2 \bigg(G_1(r) G_2(r) + h_1(r)  h_2(r) \bigg) \\
  & \qquad \qquad + {v_\perp}^2 \sin \phi \sin \varphi \bigg(G_1(r) G_2(r) - h_1(r)   h_2(r) \bigg) \Bigg]
\end{split}
\label{eq:final_full_dEdx}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
with $r^2 = \big((t-t')v_\perp + \tau' \cos \varphi - \tau \cos \phi \big)^2 + \big(\tau' \sin \varphi - \tau \sin \phi \big)^2$. 

All that is left in order to be able to evaluate $\hat{q}$ and $\diff E / \diff x$ is to provide an expression for $T$.

\subsection{``Temperature'' of the glasma}
As was outlined in Section \ref{sec:fokker-planck}, the analytically challenging $Y^\alpha$ tensor needed to compute $\diff E / \diff x$ may be obtained from $X^{\alpha \beta}$ by assuming that the Fokker-Planck equation for the probe quarks traversing the medium\footnote{\textcolor{red}{what is the rationale for even applyint the FP eq to the flasma}} should admit a Boltzmann distribution as a solution. This technique, however, requires  introducing a temperature $T$ \footnote{\textcolor{red}{of what?}} for that Boltzmann distribution. Since the glasma is not a medium in thermal equilibrium, one resorts to using the temperature of the \gls{QGP} that would be obtained if the pre-hydrodynamic stage were allowed to reach equilibrium without expanding, i.e. keeping its energy density, $\varepsilon$, constant \textcolor{red}{[cite]}. The energy density of the glasma has been computed in \cite{Guerrero_Rodr_guez_2021} within the same framework as this work -- the linearization of the Yang-Mills Equations:
\begin{equation}
	\left< \varepsilon (\tau) \right> = \left< \frac{\mathbf{E}^2 + \mathbf{B}^2}{\textcolor{red}{2}} \right>= \frac{g^2}{2} N_c (N_c - 1)^2 \int_0^{2\pi} \frac{\diff \Theta}{2 \pi} \left(1 + \cos \Theta \right) G_1(r) G_2(r)
	\label{eq:Pablos_energy_density}
\end{equation}
with $r^2 = 2 \tau^2 (1 - \cos \Theta)$.

Now, the temperature of a \gls{QGP} with the same energy density must be found. The relation between the energy density of a \gls{QGP} medium and its temperature  is \cite{Carrington_2022} \textcolor{red}{cite} 
\begin{equation}
	\varepsilon_\text{\gls{QGP}} = \frac{\pi^2}{60} \big( 4(N_c - 1)^2 + 7 N_f N_c \big) T_\text{\gls{QGP}}^4 
	\label{eq:energy_temperature}
\end{equation} 
where $N_f$ is \textcolor{red}{the number of quark flavors included in the \gls{QGP}, whose mass should be much smaller than the temperature}.

Solving Eq. (\ref{eq:energy_temperature}) for the temperature and replacing $\varepsilon_\textbf{\gls{QGP}}$ with Eq. (\ref{eq:Pablos_energy_density})'s event-averaged energy density produces an expression for $T$ to be plugged into the expression for $\diff E / \diff x$:
\begin{equation}
	T^4 = \frac{\frac{g^2}{2} N_c (N_c - 1)^2}{\frac{\pi^2}{60} \big( 4(N_c - 1)^2 + 7 N_f N_c \big)} \int_0^{2\pi} \frac{\diff \Theta}{2 \pi} \left(1 + \cos \Theta \right) G_1(r) G_2(r)
	\label{eq:final_full_T}
\end{equation}


\section{Numerically computing $\hat{q}$ and $\diff E / \diff x$}
\label{sec:numerical_computation}

Since no way was found to further analytically simplify the integrals in the formulas for $\hat{q}$ (Eq. (\ref{eq:final_full_qhat})) and $\diff E / \diff x$ (Eqs. (\ref{eq:final_full_dEdx}) and (\ref{eq:final_full_T})), numerical techniques were used in order to obtain values for these quantities. The \gls{GSL} \cite{gsl} version 2.8 for the C programming language was used to perform the calculations. 

\gls{GSL} is an open source numerical library written in C, but also available in wrappers for other programming languages. It contains a number of routines useful for numerical calculations, including the following, relevant to this work: special functions, random number generation and Monte Carlo integration.

For numerical multidimensional integration, \gls{GSL} includes the traditional Monte Carlo method, with uniform random sampling over the domain of integration, and the MISER and VEGAS adaptive algorithms, which use importance sampling and stratified sampling. After comparing values of $\hat{q}$ obtained via the three algorithms, it was found that they all produced similar results within each other's error estimates. Thus, with the objectives in mind of keeping the program simple and minimizing hard-to-interpret potential sources of error, the traditional, plain, Monte Carlo method was selected.

%In order to numerically compute a multidimensional integral in \gls{GSL}, one must write the integrand as a C function which takes, as arguments, an array of the variables to be integrated over ($t'$, $\phi$ and $\varphi$) and a pointer to a C structure containing the values of all the other parameters (e.g. $g$, $N_c$ and $v$).

%A pointer to the integrand function must then be provided to the \texttt{gsl\_monte\_plain\_integrate} function, along with the bounds of integration, the number of points to be sampled, and an already-seeded \gls{GSL} random number generator. The function then stores the value of the integral in a provided variable.

Every time the program is run, the integral is computed for several values of $t$, between $0$ and a previously set final time $t_f$, with a previously set number of steps $N_\text{steps}$. The program uses the current date as a seed for \texttt{stdlib.h}'s \texttt{rand} function, which is then used to generate seeds for \gls{GSL}'s random number generator each time an integral is calculated. The results are  then printed onto a \texttt{.txt} file headlined with the original seed (the current time) and all the parameter values. The results follow, in three columns separated by commas: the value of $t$  (in \qty{}{fm}), the integral, and the statistical error of the integral as estimated by \gls{GSL} (in  \qty{}{GeV^2/fm} for $\hat{q}$ and \qty{}{GeV/fm} for $\diff E / \diff x$).
